# Photosynthesis (Biology 101, week 4)

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae and some bacteria use to turn light energy into chemical energy.
It happens mainly in the chloroplasts of leaf cells.
The overall equation is: carbon dioxide plus water, with light energy, makes glucose and oxygen.

## Where the parts come from

Plants take in carbon dioxide from the air through small pores in the leaves called stomata.
Water is absorbed by the roots and carried up to the leaves through the xylem.
The oxygen that plants release comes from splitting water molecules, not from carbon dioxide.
Most of a plant's dry mass comes from carbon dioxide in the air, not from the soil.

## Two stages

The light dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast.
Chlorophyll absorbs mostly red and blue light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.
In the light dependent reactions, light energy splits water and produces ATP, NADPH and oxygen.
The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma, the fluid around the thylakoids.
The Calvin cycle uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar.
The enzyme RuBisCO captures carbon dioxide at the start of the Calvin cycle.
The Calvin cycle does not need light directly, but it depends on the ATP and NADPH made in the light.

## What limits the rate

The rate of photosynthesis depends on light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature.
Whichever factor is in shortest supply limits the rate, so adding more of the others does not help.
Very high temperatures slow photosynthesis down because enzymes like RuBisCO stop working well.

## Photosynthesis and respiration

Plants also do cellular respiration, all the time, day and night.
Respiration breaks glucose down to release energy and gives off carbon dioxide.
During the day photosynthesis is usually faster than respiration, so a plant takes in more carbon dioxide than it releases.
