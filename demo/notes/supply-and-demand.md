# Supply and demand (Intro to Microeconomics, week 3)

A market is where buyers and sellers trade a good, and the price coordinates what both sides do.

## Demand

The law of demand says that when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls, other things equal.
A demand curve slopes downward because buyers want less of a good as it gets more expensive.
A change in the good's own price moves along the demand curve; it does not shift the curve.
The demand curve shifts when something else changes, such as income, tastes, or the price of related goods.
Substitutes are goods used in place of each other, so if coffee gets more expensive, demand for tea rises.
Complements are goods used together, so if printers get cheaper, demand for ink rises.

## Supply

The law of supply says that when the price rises, the quantity supplied rises, other things equal.
A supply curve slopes upward because higher prices make it worth producing more.
The supply curve shifts when production costs, technology, or the number of sellers changes.
Better technology lowers costs and shifts the supply curve to the right.

## Equilibrium

The equilibrium price is where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.
If the price is above equilibrium there is a surplus, and sellers cut prices to sell what they have.
If the price is below equilibrium there is a shortage, and buyers bid the price up.
When demand rises and supply stays the same, both the equilibrium price and quantity go up.
When supply rises and demand stays the same, the equilibrium price falls and the quantity goes up.

## Price controls

A price ceiling is a legal maximum price, like rent control.
A price ceiling set below equilibrium causes a shortage, because more people want to buy than sellers offer.
A price floor is a legal minimum price, like a minimum wage.
A price floor set above equilibrium causes a surplus.
